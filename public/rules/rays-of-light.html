How a round works

Rays of Light is played inside the VRO app. You build a robot, program it, and run it on the field.

Practice runs are unlimited. Change the robot, change the program, run it again as often as you like.

A Competition Round is the one that counts. The field is randomised, the clock starts, and your program runs. When you are finished you press Stop Round. The app scores the field exactly as it stands at that moment and records how long the round took.

Your result goes straight to the global leaderboard, ranked by score first and by time as the tiebreaker.

A season runs for one year. When it closes, the top three are placed permanently in the VRO Hall of Fame.

The field

The Light Source, the VRO ROBOTRIAL square at bottom centre, is the start and the finish. Every round begins with the robot inside it.

A coloured border or line belongs to the area it encloses. An object resting on the line of an area counts as inside that area. Completely inside means the object sits fully within the area, touching the line included. Touching means it overlaps the area or its line without being fully inside.

Rules

  1. One account, one competitor. Your leaderboard entry is your own work.
  2. Missions may be attempted in any order, and you may skip any of them.
  3. The robot starts completely inside the Light Source, and is whatever you build in the configurator.
  4. Scoring is based on where things are when you press Stop Round. An object placed correctly and later knocked out of place scores by its final position.
  5. Only one object scores in a single target area.
  6. On the randomised missions, colour matching is tiered: full points for a correct placement, half for a correct-colour object only touching its area, and a quarter for an object placed in the wrong colour or orientation.
  7. Time is recorded for every Competition Round and breaks ties between equal scores.

Collect the four Light Cells from the lower left and carry each one to the Screen Pixel holding a square of the same colour. The colours are shuffled every round, so the robot has to read colour to find each target.

Align the Prism M2 · 50 pts

Take the Prism from the Spectrum Source at the upper right and deliver it to the Spectrum Filter at the upper left. Then turn it so its green–red face points at the green–red bands and its yellow–blue face points at the yellow–blue bands. Which pair sits on top is decided when the round starts.
